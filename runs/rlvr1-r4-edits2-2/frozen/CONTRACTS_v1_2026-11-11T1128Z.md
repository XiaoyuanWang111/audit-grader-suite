# Contracts for the 47 instruction checks of RLVR1 instruction following

Written from the instruction texts that the model sees (the user prompt of each row), before reading
any grader or checker code. Every one of the 28,219 constraints in `case/data/rlvr1_instruction_following.jsonl`
carries one of the 47 texts below (found case-insensitively in its prompt, trailing period optional;
`audit/scripts/templates2.py` finds all 28,219). Placeholders in braces are the constraint's kwargs.
The instruction texts sit in the prompt as a list after a header such as "Make sure to adhere to these
requirements:", or inline joined with ", " and "and"; the wording of each instruction is the same.

## Graded text

- The graded text is the model's visible answer. For a reasoning model it is the text after the last
  end-of-reasoning marker (`</think>` for Qwen3); a separate reasoning field is never graded.
- A response whose reasoning never closes (a `<think>` opened by the chat template or by the model, and no
  `</think>` after it) is an extraction failure. It fails every check, and is counted separately.
- A non-reasoning model's text is graded whole. (A literal `<think>` in a non-reasoning model's answer is
  answer text; see D-literal-think.)
- "Your response", "your answer", "your entire response" and "entire output" all mean the graded text.

## Shared definitions (each one names the decision entry that holds its open question)

- **Word** (D-word): a maximal run of letters, digits, and apostrophes or hyphens between letters or digits,
  so `don't`, `well-known` and `2024` are one word each. Markup characters (`*`, `_`, `#`, `` ` ``, `<`, `>`,
  `[`, `]`, quotes) and punctuation are not part of a word. A token with no letter or digit is not a word.
- **Word match** (D-keyword-match): a given word matches a word of the response when they are equal ignoring
  case. For keyword checks, the keyword matches inside a response word that is split at hyphens and
  apostrophes (`food's` contains `food`, `foods` does not).
- **Sentence** (D-sentence): a sentence ends at a run of `.`, `!` or `?` that is followed by whitespace or
  the end of the text, and at the end of a line or paragraph. A period in an abbreviation (`e.g.`, `Dr.`),
  a decimal number or a URL does not end a sentence. A sentence has at least one word. A list item or a
  heading without final punctuation is one sentence.
- **Paragraph** (two-line-break checks, D-paragraph-separator): the graded text split at every run of two or
  more line breaks (lines that hold only whitespace count as blank); leading and trailing blank lines are
  ignored.
- **Markdown divider** (D-divider): a line that holds only `***` (surrounding whitespace allowed).
- **Comparison words**: "at least N" means count >= N; "less than N" means count < N; "at most N" means
  count <= N; "exactly N" means count == N.
- **Empty answer** (D-empty): a graded text with no word fails every check, including prohibitions such as
  "refrain from the use of any commas".

## Contracts

Each entry: the instruction text, then Pass / Fail, then near-miss notes and the decision entries it uses.

### change_case:capital_word_frequency
Text: "In your response, words with all capital letters should appear {capital_relation} {capital_frequency} times."
- Count the words whose letters are all uppercase (a word with at least one cased letter and no lowercase
  letter; digits allowed, so `COVID-19` counts). Every occurrence counts, repeats included.
- Pass: the count satisfies the relation. Fail: otherwise.
- D-capital-word: whether one-letter words `I` and `A` count (proposed: they count).

### change_case:english_capital
Text: "Your entire response should be in English, and in all capital letters."
- Pass: the graded text contains no lowercase letter, and it is written in English.
- Fail: any lowercase letter anywhere (code, URLs and quotes included); or the text is not in English.
- D-english: what "in English" admits (proposed: the text's language is English; names, numbers and
  short foreign terms are allowed; text in another language or script, or with no words, fails).

### change_case:english_lowercase
Text: "Your entire response should be in English, and in all lowercase letters. No capital letters are allowed."
- Pass: no uppercase letter anywhere, and the text is in English (D-english).
- Fail: any uppercase letter, or not English.

### combination:two_responses
Text: "Give two different responses. Responses and only responses should be separated by 6 asterisk symbols: ******."
- Pass: the graded text contains the separator `******` exactly once; the run of asterisks there is exactly
  six long; the text before it and the text after it each contain a word; and the two parts differ after
  trimming whitespace.
- Fail: no separator; two or more separators; a run of 7 or more asterisks as the only separator; an
  empty side; two identical sides.
- D-two-responses: how different the two responses must be (proposed: not identical after trimming
  whitespace).

### copy:repeat_phrase
Text: "Repeat the phrase {phrase} exactly {small_n} times, transforming it slightly each time by replacing only one word in the center of the phrase."
- A variant is a run of words with the phrase's word count that equals the phrase in every word but one
  (case-insensitive), and the changed word is not the first or the last word of the phrase.
- Pass: the graded text holds exactly small_n variants, and the unchanged phrase does not appear.
- Fail: fewer or more variants; a variant that changes two words or an edge word; an unchanged copy.
- D-repeat-center: which word positions are "the center" (proposed: any word but the first and the last).
- D-repeat-original: whether an unchanged copy of the phrase may appear in addition (proposed: no; each
  repetition is transformed).

### count:count_unique  (judged by the LLM judge in task curation)
Text: "Only use unique words in your response, no word should be repeated!"
- Pass: no word occurs twice, comparing words ignoring case. Fail: some word occurs twice or more.
- D-count-unique-case: whether `The` and `the` are the same word (proposed: yes).

### count:counting_composition
Text: "Write 3 paragraphs, delimited by the markdown divider: * * *, with exactly {n_sent} sentences each, with exactly {n_words} words in each sentence."
- Pass: the graded text splits at lines holding only `* * *` into exactly 3 parts, each with a word; each
  part has exactly n_sent sentences; each sentence has exactly n_words words.
- Fail: otherwise.

### count:lowercase_counting
Text: "In your response, all lowercase words should appear at most {N} times."
- Pass: every word written entirely in lowercase occurs at most N times written that way.
- Fail: some lowercase word occurs N+1 times or more.
- D-lowercase-counting: whether capitalized occurrences (`The`) count toward the lowercase word (`the`)
  (proposed: no, only lowercase occurrences count).

### detectable_content:number_placeholders
Text: "The response must contain at least {num_placeholders} placeholders represented by square brackets, such as [address]."
- Pass: the number of square-bracketed spans `[...]` with non-blank content and no line break is at least N.
- Fail: fewer. `[]` and `[ ]` do not count.
- D-placeholder: whether bracketed spans that are not placeholders, such as markdown link text `[text](url)`
  or citation marks `[1]`, count (proposed: every bracketed span counts; the text names only the form).

### detectable_content:postscript
Text: "At the end of your response, please explicitly add a postscript starting with {postscript_marker}"
- Pass: a line of the graded text starts with the marker (after optional markdown emphasis such as `**`),
  the marker is followed by postscript text, and the postscript runs to the end of the response.
- Fail: no marker; the marker only mid-line; the postscript followed by other content that is not part of
  it (for example a further section); a different marker (`PS`, `P.S` for `P.S.`).
- D-postscript: whether the postscript must be the last part of the response, or only present (proposed:
  last part; a further unrelated block after it fails).
- Marker match is case-sensitive and exact; `P.P.S.` starts with `P.P.S`.

### detectable_format:bigram_wrapping  (judged by the LLM judge in task curation)
Text: "Wrap every word bigram in double angular brackets, such as <<I am>> <<at home>> <<with my>> <<cute dog>>."
- Pass: every word of the graded text is inside a `<<...>>` group, and each group holds exactly two words.
- Fail: a word outside any group; a group of one or three or more words.
- D-bigram-odd: with an odd word count, whether one group of one word is allowed (proposed: one final
  single-word group is allowed).

### detectable_format:constrained_response
Text: "Answer with one of the following options: ('My answer is yes.', 'My answer is no.', 'My answer is maybe.')"
- Pass: the graded text contains exactly one of the three option strings, verbatim (case and period
  included), and contains no other option.
- Fail: no option; two different options.
- D-constrained-exact: whether other text may surround the option (proposed: yes; the alternative is that
  the graded text, trimmed, is exactly the option).

### detectable_format:json_format
Text: "Entire output should be wrapped in JSON format. You can use markdown ticks such as ```."
- Pass: the graded text, trimmed, is valid JSON (RFC 8259), or is one markdown code fence (an opening line
  ```` ``` ```` or ```` ```json ````, a closing line ```` ``` ````) whose content is valid JSON, with nothing
  outside the fence.
- Fail: invalid JSON (single quotes, trailing commas, comments, NaN); any text outside the JSON or the
  fence; two JSON documents.
- D-json-value: whether a bare JSON scalar such as `"hello"` or `42` is "wrapped in JSON format" (proposed:
  any valid JSON value passes).

### detectable_format:multiple_sections
Text: "Your response must have {num_sections} sections. Mark the beginning of each section with {section_spliter} X, such as:\n{section_spliter} 1\n[content of section 1]\n{section_spliter} 2\n[content of section 2]"
- A marker is a line that starts (after optional markdown such as `#`, `**`) with the splitter word exactly
  as given, a space and a number.
- Pass: exactly num_sections markers, numbered 1 to N in order, each followed by section content.
- Fail: a different number of markers; a section with no content.
- D-sections: (a) whether text before the first marker is allowed (proposed: a title or a one-line
  preamble is allowed); (b) whether the splitter's case must match (`Section 1` for `SECTION`; proposed:
  must match, the text gives the exact form); (c) whether the numbers must run 1 to N (proposed: yes).

### detectable_format:number_bullet_lists
Text: "Your answer must contain exactly {num_bullets} bullet points. Use the markdown bullet points such as:\n* This is point 1. \n* This is point 2"
- A bullet point is a line that starts, after optional indentation, with `*`, `-` or `+` and a space, and
  then text. A line starting with `**` (bold) or a numbered item (`1.`) is not a bullet point; a markdown
  divider is not a bullet point.
- Pass: exactly num_bullets bullet points. Fail: any other count.
- D-bullets: (a) whether `-` and `+` bullets count, since the example shows `*` (proposed: yes, they are
  markdown bullet points); (b) whether nested sub-bullets count (proposed: yes, every bullet point counts).

### detectable_format:number_highlighted_sections
Text: "Highlight at least {num_highlights} sections in your answer with markdown, i.e. *highlighted section*."
- A highlighted section is a markdown emphasis span `*text*` with non-blank text on one line. A bullet
  marker `* ` at a line start is not a highlight.
- Pass: at least N highlighted sections. Fail: fewer.
- D-highlight: whether bold `**text**` counts as a highlighted section (proposed: yes, bold is markdown
  highlighting; the alternative is that only the single-asterisk form shown counts).

### detectable_format:sentence_hyphens
Text: "All sentences must be connected using hyphens, with no spaces between them."
- Pass: every boundary between two consecutive sentences is a hyphen `-` with no whitespace (and no line
  break) on either side of it: `One idea.-Another idea.-A third.`
- Fail: two sentences separated by whitespace or a line break without a hyphen; a hyphen with a space
  next to it at a sentence boundary.
- D-hyphens-single: whether a one-sentence response passes (proposed: yes, nothing needs connecting).

### detectable_format:square_brackets
Text: "Enclose every word in your response within square brackets."
- Pass: each word of the graded text is inside its own pair of square brackets, `[Hello] [world]`;
  punctuation may sit inside or outside the brackets.
- Fail: a word outside brackets; several words in one bracket pair.
- D-square-brackets: whether one bracket pair around many words (`[Hello world]`) satisfies "every word
  ... within square brackets" (proposed: no, each word gets its own brackets).

### detectable_format:title
Text: "Your answer must contain a title, wrapped in double angular brackets, such as <<poem of joy>>."
- Pass: the graded text contains `<<...>>` with non-blank content on one line.
- Fail: no such span; only `<<>>` or `<< >>`.
- D-title: whether the title must be at the start of the answer (proposed: anywhere; the text says
  "contain").

### first_word:first_word_answer
Text: "The first word of your response should be the word {first_word}."
- Pass: the first word of the graded text (leading whitespace, markup and punctuation skipped) matches
  first_word ignoring case.
- Fail: any other first word; an empty answer.
- D-first-last-case: whether capitalization must match (proposed: case is ignored; sentence-initial
  capitals are normal).

### first_word:first_word_sent
Text: "The first word of each sentence should be the word {first_word}."
- Pass: there is at least one sentence, and the first word of every sentence matches first_word
  ignoring case.
- Fail: some sentence starts with another word. Uses D-sentence and D-first-last-case.

### keywords:existence
Text: "Include keywords {keywords} in the response." (keywords shown as a Python list, such as ['food', 'poetry'])
- Pass: every keyword occurs (D-keyword-match). Fail: some keyword is missing.

### keywords:forbidden_words
Text: "Do not include keywords {forbidden_words} in the response."
- Pass: no keyword occurs (D-keyword-match). Fail: some keyword occurs.
- Under D-keyword-match's proposal, `laws` does not include `law`; the alternative reading
  (substring) fails it.

### keywords:frequency
Text: "In your response, the word {keyword} should appear {relation} {frequency} times."
- Count the occurrences of keyword (D-keyword-match). Pass: the count satisfies the relation.

### keywords:keyword_specific_position
Text: "Include keyword {keyword} in the {n}-th sentence, as the {m}-th word of that sentence."
- Pass: the graded text has at least n sentences, the n-th sentence has at least m words, and its m-th
  word matches keyword (ignoring case and attached punctuation). Fail: otherwise. Uses D-sentence, D-word.

### keywords:letter_frequency
Text: "In your response, the letter {letter} should appear {let_relation} {let_frequency} times."
- Count the occurrences of the letter, upper and lower case together; accented letters (`é`) are not the
  letter. Pass: the count satisfies the relation.
- D-letter-case: whether uppercase occurrences count (proposed: yes).

### keywords:no_adjacent_consecutive
Text: "No two adjacent words can start with consecutive letters of the alphabet."
- For each pair of adjacent words (punctuation and line breaks between them ignored), take each word's
  first character, lowercased. Pass: no pair's first letters are consecutive in a-z.
- Fail: some pair such as `a big` or `big apple`.
- D-consecutive-order: whether reverse order (`big apple`: b then a) also violates (proposed: yes; b and a
  are consecutive letters).

### keywords:palindrome
Text: "Include a palindrome in your response."
- Pass: the graded text contains a word of three or more letters that reads the same backwards ignoring
  case (`level`, `noon`, `did`), or a run of words that does so ignoring case, spaces and punctuation.
- Fail: none.
- D-palindrome-length: whether one- and two-letter words (`a`, `I`, `oo`) count (proposed: no).

### keywords:start_end
Text: "Start and end your response with the same word (do not write anything after the last word, not even punctuation)."
- Pass: the first word and the last word of the graded text match ignoring case, and nothing but
  whitespace follows the last word.
- Fail: different words; any character other than whitespace after the last word (a period, a quote,
  `**`).
- D-start-end-lead: whether leading punctuation or markup before the first word is allowed (proposed:
  allowed; the text restricts only what follows the last word).

### keywords:word_count_different_numbers
Text: "In your response, the word {keyword} should appear {frequency} times." (the kwargs also hold a
relation, `at least` or `less than`, which the text does not show)
- Pass: keyword occurs exactly `frequency` times (D-keyword-match).
- Fail: any other count.
- D-word-count-exact: whether "should appear N times" means exactly N (proposed) or at least N. No
  reading of the text means "less than N": under it, N occurrences fail and zero occurrences pass.

### keywords:word_once
Text: "Include keyword {keyword} in your response."
- Pass: keyword occurs at least once (D-keyword-match). Fail: it does not occur. Two or more occurrences
  pass: the text only asks to include it.

### language:response_language  (judged by the LLM judge in task curation)
Text: "Your ENTIRE response should be in {language name} language, no other language is allowed."
- Pass: the graded text is written in that language throughout. Names, numbers, code and widely used
  technical terms are allowed. Fail: a sentence or more in another language, or the wrong language.
- D-language-mixed: whether a short phrase in another language fails (proposed: a sentence or more fails).

### last_word:last_word_answer
Text: "The last word of your response should be the word {last_word}."
- Pass: the last word of the graded text (trailing whitespace, punctuation and markup skipped) matches
  last_word ignoring case (D-first-last-case). Fail: otherwise.

### last_word:last_word_sent
Text: "The last word of each sentence, before punctuation, should be the word {last_word}."
- Pass: there is at least one sentence, and every sentence's last word (before its final punctuation)
  matches last_word ignoring case. Fail: some sentence ends with another word. Uses D-sentence.

### length_constraints:nth_paragraph_first_word
Text: "There should be {num_paragraphs} paragraphs. Paragraphs and only paragraphs are separated with each other by two new lines as if it was '\n\n' in python. Paragraph {nth_paragraph} must start with word {first_word}."
- Pass: the graded text has exactly num_paragraphs paragraphs (two-line-break split), and the first word
  of paragraph nth_paragraph matches first_word ignoring case (leading markup and punctuation skipped).
- Fail: another paragraph count; another first word.
- D-paragraph-separator: whether three or more line breaks form one separator (proposed) or a separator
  plus an empty paragraph.

### length_constraints:number_paragraphs
Text: "There should be {num_paragraphs} paragraphs. Paragraphs are separated with the markdown divider: ***"
- Pass: the graded text split at markdown dividers gives exactly num_paragraphs parts that contain a word.
- Fail: another count.
- D-divider: (a) whether only a line holding `***` is a divider, or also `***` inside a line (proposed:
  only the line); (b) whether a part with no word, such as a divider at the very start or end, counts
  as a paragraph (proposed: it does not count, and it does not fail the check).
- Blank lines inside a part do not create paragraphs here: the text names the divider as the separator.

### length_constraints:number_sentences
Text: "Your response should contain {relation} {num_sentences} sentences."
- Count sentences (D-sentence). Pass: the count satisfies the relation.

### length_constraints:number_words
Text: "Answer with {relation} {num_words} words."
- Count words (D-word). Pass: the count satisfies the relation.

### letters:letter_counting  (judged by the LLM judge in task curation)
Text: "Answer with {relation} {N} letters." (N is 2 or 3)
- Count the letters (alphabetic characters of any script) in the graded text. Pass: the count satisfies
  the relation.
- D-letters-count: whether "letters" means alphabetic characters (proposed) or letters of the alphabet a
  to z only.

### letters:letter_counting2
Text: "In your response, the letter {letter} should appear {let_relation} {let_frequency} times."
- Same contract as keywords:letter_frequency (D-letter-case).

### paragraphs:paragraphs
Text: "There should be 2 paragraphs. Paragraphs are separated with the markdown divider: ***"
- Same contract as length_constraints:number_paragraphs with num_paragraphs = 2.

### paragraphs:paragraphs2
Text: "There should be 2 paragraphs. Paragraphs and only paragraphs are separated with each other by two line breaks."
- Pass: exactly 2 paragraphs (two-line-break split, D-paragraph-separator). Fail: any other count.

### punctuation:no_comma
Text: "In your entire response, refrain from the use of any commas."
- Pass: no comma character: `,` (U+002C), `，` (U+FF0C), `،` (U+060C). Fail: any.
- D-punct-variants: whether non-ASCII forms count (proposed: the listed forms are commas; the ideographic
  enumeration comma `、` also counts).

### punctuation:punctuation_dot
Text: "In your entire response, refrain from the use of . (i.e. dots) as punctuation and in general."
- Pass: no dot: `.` (U+002E) anywhere, including decimals, URLs and abbreviations, and no `…` (U+2026),
  `．` (U+FF0E) or `。` (U+3002). Fail: any.
- D-punct-variants: whether `…` and `。` are dots (proposed: yes).

### punctuation:punctuation_exclamation
Text: "In your entire response, refrain from the use of ! (i.e. exclamation marks) as punctuation and in general."
- Pass: no `!` (U+0021) and no `！` (U+FF01) anywhere. Fail: any. (D-punct-variants.)

### startend:end_checker
Text: "Finish your response with this exact phrase {end_phrase}. No other words should follow this phrase."
- Pass: the graded text, with trailing whitespace removed, ends with end_phrase exactly (case and
  punctuation included).
- Fail: the phrase missing or changed; any word after it.
- D-end-phrase: (a) whether trailing markup or quotes after the phrase (`**`, `"`) are allowed, since they
  are not words (proposed: allowed when they close markup opened around the phrase; other characters
  fail); (b) whether case must match (proposed: yes, "exact phrase").

### startend:quotation
Text: "Wrap your entire response with double quotation marks."
- Pass: the graded text, trimmed, starts with a double quotation mark and ends with one, and holds a word
  between them.
- Fail: otherwise.
- D-quotation-typographic: whether typographic quotes (U+201C, U+201D) count (proposed: yes).

| D-capital-word | `I` and `A` count as all-capital words | Only words of two or more letters | `I SAW IT`: 3 / 2 | A |
